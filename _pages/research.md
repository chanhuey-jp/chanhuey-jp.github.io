## General Scope

My research focuses on **reversible gas–solid thermochemical reactions**, which can be generally expressed as:

**A(s) + ΔH ⇌ B(s) + C(g)** (Eq.1)

Under specific temperature and pressure conditions, solid A decomposes into solid B and gas C (e.g., O<sub>2</sub>, CO<sub>2</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>O). This forward reaction is typically **endothermic**, absorbing heat from the surroundings. When the reaction conditions—temperature, the partial pressure of C, or both—are altered, solid B reacts with gas C to re-form solid A. This reverse reaction is generally **exothermic**, releasing heat to the environment.

## Typical Reaction Systems

Based on this reversible behavior, gas–solid thermochemical reactions play an essential role in **energy storage** and **gas separation/recovery**. The systems I currently focus on include the H<sub>2</sub>O system (Eq.2), O<sub>2</sub> system (Eq.3), and CO<sub>2</sub> system (Eq.4). These reactions can be applied to **Thermochemical Energy Storage (TCES)** as well as **Carbon Dioxide Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS)**.

- **Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub>(s) + ΔH ⇌ CaO(s) + H<sub>2</sub>O(g)** (Eq.2)
- **Co<sub>3</sub>O<sub>4</sub>(s) + ΔH ⇌ 3CoO(s) + 1/2 O<sub>2</sub>(g)** (Eq.3)
- **CaCO<sub>3</sub>(s) + ΔH ⇌ CaO(s) + CO<sub>2</sub>(g)** (Eq.4)

For the O<sub>2</sub> system, the decomposition product B can also function as an **oxygen carrier (OC)**, enabling further applications in **Chemical Looping Combustion (CLC)** and **Thermochemical Water Splitting (TWS)** for hydrogen production.

In TCES, the primary metrics include **gravimetric and volumetric energy storage density** (kJ·kg<sup>−1</sup>, kJ·L<sup>−1</sup>).  
In CCUS, the key concern is the **CO<sub>2</sub> uptake capacity** (kg-CO<sub>2</sub>·kg<sup>−1</sup>-abs, kg-CO<sub>2</sub>·L<sup>−1</sup>-abs).  
Despite the different application targets, both systems share common evaluation factors such as **reaction rate, cycling stability, toxicity, and cost**.

## Research Methodology and Core Concerns

{% include research-pathway.html %}

### 1. Materials Development
Develop metal hydroxides (Me(OH)<sub>x</sub>), oxides (MeO<sub>x</sub>) and carbonates (MeCO<sub>x</sub>) for high reaction capacity and cycling stability. Composition tuning and pore-structure engineering balance reactivity, heat transfer and cost.

### 2. Reaction Kinetics Analysis
Use TGA and DSC to characterize reaction behaviour and determine kinetic parameters. The resulting models provide reaction-rate inputs for reactor design.

### 3. Reaction Module and Reactor Design
Design structured modules, including honeycombs and porous foams, to improve heat and mass transfer while limiting pressure drop. Reactor development also addresses sintering, agglomeration and cycling performance.

### 4. System Integration and Optimization
Apply energy and mass balances to integrate reactors with waste heat recovery, solar-thermal systems and CO<sub>2</sub> capture processes.

## New Approaches (AI for Chemical Engineering)

With advancements in **computational chemistry (e.g., DFT)**, **numerical simulation**, and **machine learning (ML)**, several new methodologies have emerged:

- Using DFT to compute material stability, reaction enthalpy, oxygen vacancy formation energies, and other key properties  
- Constructing hybrid datasets combining DFT calculations and experimental measurements to train ML models for high-throughput materials discovery  
- Developing soft-sensor models that integrate numerical simulation and experimental data, enabling real-time prediction of variables that cannot be directly measured by physical sensors

These approaches significantly accelerate research progress and practical deployment of gas–solid reversible thermochemical technologies in **energy systems** and **carbon-neutral applications**.
